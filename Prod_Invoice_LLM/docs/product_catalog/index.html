The problem

Two approaches. One shared failure point.

Both produce a number nobody actually verified.

Template OCR — ABBYY, Kofax
"Reads positions, not documents."

A supplier moves a field two centimetres and the template stops matching. Character confusion between "O" and "0" produces an invoice number that looks right to a person but is a different string to the ERP — so the duplicate check that should fire never does.

Failure mode: position drift → silent extraction error → no duplicate flag fires.
General-purpose AI / LLM extraction
"Produces plausible numbers."

A language model asked for a total generates the most probable sequence of digits. When line items and tax don't quite reconcile, it produces a figure that makes the arithmetic look right. It never signals uncertainty, so the error arrives formatted exactly like a correct answer.

Failure mode: confident wrong answer, styled identically to a correct one.

By role

Different teams. One shared outcome:
no invoice that bypasses verification.

CFO & Internal Audit
Own: fiduciary control and exposure

Testing moves from a sample to the whole population. Deterministic arithmetic and duplicate interception cover every invoice, not a random 30.

AP Operations
Own: getting documents in and processed

Downloading and filing stops being a job. Autopilot Sync collects from mail and cloud drives; extraction runs without a person in the loop.

Financial Controller
Own: close accuracy and accruals

Unmatched receipts shrink to genuine exceptions. Three-way matching filters out the structural mismatches before they reach the controller's queue.

Procurement
Own: order and receipt integrity

Matching stops arriving in a buyer's inbox. Part matching tolerates supplier abbreviations; partial deliveries reconcile natively without manual intervention.

Treasury & FP&A
Own: spend visibility, supplier behaviour

Price movement is visible without a data request. SAGE queries the entire invoice history in plain English — vendor price creep surfaces before it compounds.

IT & Architecture
Own: risk, isolation, change safety

A finance system that does not become their problem. Structural tenant separation, rule replay before promotion, and rollback capability cover the integration risk.

Where this doesn't apply

When InvoiceEQ is the wrong tool.

Every vendor document answers yes to everything, which is precisely why buyers discount all of it. Making these statements now is the only reason to believe the rest.

01
You don't raise purchase orders. Three-way matching needs a PO and goods receipt; without them you get verification and duplicate control — not the core value driver.
02
Under ~500 invoices per month. A competent person is cheaper than any software at that volume.
03
Already live on Coupa or Ariba. Displacing a working procure-to-pay suite is a renewal-driven conversation, not a capability-gap one.
04
Policy mandates on-premise hardware. This is a cloud architecture. Regional cloud can be discussed; on-prem hardware cannot.
