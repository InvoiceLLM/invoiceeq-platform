Manufacturing
Multi-entity manufacturer
38,000 invoices/mo · 4,200 suppliers · 3 countries
What breaks today

Three countries means three tax regimes — VAT rates, reverse-charge rules, and withholding requirements vary by jurisdiction. No individual AP team member knows all three. Invoice processing is routed by country of origin, but routing logic fails when a supplier issues from an unexpected entity. The result: invoices processed under the wrong tax treatment, requiring manual correction at close.

How InvoiceEQ addresses it

Tax jurisdiction is read per document from the supplier address and registration details extracted by NOVA — not assigned by routing logic. SENTINEL applies the applicable rate for that jurisdiction when verifying totals. A UK VAT invoice and a German reverse-charge invoice are verified against different rules automatically, not by manual routing assignment.

Automotive supply chain
Tier-1 automotive supplier
900 suppliers · 60,000 part numbers · JIT delivery
What breaks today

A supplier's invoice refers to part "WGT-A3-REV2" against a PO line referencing "Widget Assembly A3 (Revision 2)." Exact-string matching fails. A buyer manually confirms they refer to the same item and clears the exception — 60,000 part numbers creates a full-time exception queue. JIT delivery means partial receipts are routine: a PO for 500 units ships in three tranches; each tranche invoice creates a mismatch against the full-PO quantity.

How InvoiceEQ addresses it

Vector similarity matching aligns supplier part descriptions to PO line items without requiring exact string equality. "WGT-A3-REV2" matches "Widget Assembly A3 (Revision 2)" within a configurable similarity threshold. Partial delivery invoicing reconciles against dock receipts, not full PO quantities — a tranche of 180 units against a 500-unit PO is matched against the corresponding goods receipt, not flagged as a quantity exception.

Construction & EPC
Infrastructure & EPC contractor
Subcontractor billing · progress claims · retention
What breaks today

Retention / holdback is a standard construction billing practice: the client withholds 5–10% of each progress claim until practical completion. Arithmetic verification systems treat the retention deduction as an unexplained discrepancy — the invoice total does not equal line items minus the deduction as computed. The result: every construction invoice becomes an exception, regardless of correctness. The exception queue becomes unmanageable.

How InvoiceEQ addresses it

Retention and holdback amounts are recognised as document structure — a specific line item type with defined treatment — not as arithmetic failures. The verification engine rebuilds the total including the retention deduction as a negative line item against the gross amount. A correctly-drawn progress claim with 5% retention verifies successfully and posts without manual review.

Regulated industry
Pharmaceutical distribution
Regulated supply chain · high SKU count · audit sampling
What breaks today

Audit reviews sample approximately 30 invoices from a monthly population of 45,000. The 44,970 unchecked invoices represent an unquantified financial exposure — any systematic error that bypasses the sample is invisible until an external audit or a supplier dispute surfaces it. Regulators increasingly view statistical sampling as insufficient for controlled-substance supply chains.

How InvoiceEQ addresses it

100% population verification: every invoice is processed through deterministic arithmetic verification and three-way matching, regardless of value. The audit trail for every invoice is complete and immutable — an auditor can pull any processed invoice and see exactly what was extracted, what was verified, and which purchase order and goods receipt it matched, without an IT data extract.

Shared services
Group shared services centre
14 legal entities · one AP team · cross-entity risk
What breaks today

Onboarding a new legal entity into the shared services centre takes a quarter — new template sets for that entity's supplier base, configuration changes in the ERP, and training. During onboarding, the entity processes manually. Cross-entity data access risk exists in shared platforms where tenant isolation is an application flag rather than a structural property — one misconfiguration exposes all entities' invoice data.

How InvoiceEQ addresses it

Adding a new legal entity is tenant configuration, not a migration or template project. The new entity inherits the global rule set immediately; supplier-specific tuning is layered on top as invoices process. Cross-entity isolation is structural — a mandatory tenant predicate at the ORM layer means entity A's team cannot access entity B's invoice data by construction, not by policy.

Retail & hospitality
Multi-site retail & hospitality
800 sites · utilities, freight, small suppliers
What breaks today

A £42 utility invoice from a small regional supplier doesn't get checked — the review threshold means low-value invoices process automatically without verification. A supplier billing an extra £42 per site per month across 800 sites is a £400,800 annual exposure that sits below every alert threshold and accumulates for years. Small-value systematic errors are the class most likely to be invisible in sampling-based review.

How InvoiceEQ addresses it

Every invoice is verified regardless of value. The arithmetic check on a £42 utility invoice costs the same as one on a £420,000 construction claim — there is no threshold below which verification is skipped. Systematic overbilling across a large supplier portfolio becomes detectable the month it starts, not after a year of aggregate drift.
